Best restaurants in Fukuoka

Fukuoka is the best food city in Japan per yen. It is the birthplace of tonkotsu ramen, the home of motsunabe and mizutaki hot pots, the yakitori capital by number of shops, and the last city in Japan with a real street-stall (yatai) culture, where you sit at a plastic counter on the Nakasu riverbank eating ramen and gyoza next to strangers. Hakata and Tenjin are the two centers, fifteen minutes apart on foot.

Fukuoka's restaurant scene is tight enough that Tabelog ratings work beautifully: the top-rated tonkotsu shops are all within a short walk of Hakata station, and the 3.6+ motsunabe houses are a short taxi ride from Tenjin. TabeDog shows which yatai and ramen shops are open right now, which matters in a city where the best bowls are eaten at midnight.

Where to eat in Fukuoka, by neighborhood

  • Hakata the station district, home of the original tonkotsu shops and Hakata udon
  • Tenjin the downtown: department stores, yakitori alleys and the Daimyo restaurant streets
  • Nakasu the riverside entertainment island and the yatai street-stall row
  • Daimyo & Imaizumi young, dense neighborhoods of izakaya, cafes and natural-wine bars west of Tenjin
  • Yakuin quiet residential streets with some of the highest-rated small restaurants in the city
  • Nagahama the fish market and the original Nagahama-style thin-noodle ramen shops

What to eat in Fukuoka

  • Hakata tonkotsu ramen creamy pork-bone broth, thin noodles, and kaedama noodle refills
  • Motsunabe beef offal hot pot with garlic, chives and cabbage
  • Mizutaki chicken hot pot in a milky collagen broth
  • Yakitori (butabara) Fukuoka grills pork belly skewers alongside chicken, with a cabbage salad
  • Hakata udon deliberately soft noodles with burdock tempura
  • Mentaiko spicy cod roe, Fukuoka's signature souvenir and rice topping

Fukuoka restaurant FAQ

What food is Fukuoka famous for?

Hakata tonkotsu ramen, Motsunabe, Mizutaki, Yakitori (butabara), Hakata udon, Mentaiko. Creamy pork-bone broth, thin noodles, and kaedama noodle refills.

Where should I eat in Fukuoka?

Hakata for the station district, home of the original tonkotsu shops and Hakata udon; Tenjin for the downtown: department stores, yakitori alleys and the Daimyo restaurant streets; Nakasu for the riverside entertainment island and the yatai street-stall row. The pages for ramen, hot pot & motsunabe, yakitori in Fukuoka go neighborhood by neighborhood.

What is a good Tabelog rating in Fukuoka?

Is Tabelog available in English for Fukuoka?

Tabelog has a limited English site, but most listings, reviews and the useful details (hours, prices, payment methods) are only in Japanese. TabeDog shows Fukuoka's restaurants on an English map with those details translated, using Tabelog's own ratings.

How do I avoid tourist traps in Fukuoka?

Use the Tabelog score, not the sign. Restaurants with big English signs and touts near Hakata typically rate 3.0 or lower; the ones locals queue for rate 3.4 and up and are often a street or two away. TabeDog puts both on the same map so the difference is visible.
